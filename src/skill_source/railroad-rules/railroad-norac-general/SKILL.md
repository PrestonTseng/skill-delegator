# NORAC Operating Rules — General Rules

## When to Use
Questions about NORAC General Rules (employee conduct, drugs/alcohol, safety, fouling tracks),
time/job briefing rules, miscellaneous signals (flags/lights, hand signals, horn/bell, blue signal,
markers), or equipment inspection rules. Full raw text in `references/norac-general-rules.md`.

For personnel duties (Dispatchers 900–905, Operators 920–928, Engineers/Conductors, Yardmasters)
see `railroad-norac-comms-personnel`. For train movement see `railroad-norac-train-movement`.

---

## General Rules A–V (Key Rules)

**A. Required Books:** Employees must have rulebook, timetable, SIs, and supplements accessible
while on duty.

**B. Rules and Special Instructions:** Good Faith Challenge procedure — employee may challenge an
instruction believed unsafe. Three-step: (1) Challenge, (2) Resolution attempt, (3) Final
verification. Employee must comply if resolution is not achieved and safety is not at imminent risk.

**C. Required Examinations:** Employees must pass operating rules exam at required intervals.

**D. Employee Conduct / E. Prohibited Behaviors:** Must comply with all rules; prohibited behaviors
include insubordination, sleeping on duty, unauthorized use of company property.

**F. Reporting Unusual Occurrences:** Must promptly report rule violations, injuries, unusual
conditions.

**G. Drugs and Alcohol:** Use of alcohol or drugs while subject to or on duty is prohibited.

**Q. Hours of Service:** Must comply with applicable Hours of Service law requirements.

**S. Safety; Following the Safe Course:** When in doubt, take the safe course. Stop and communicate
with proper authority.

**V. Fouling Tracks:** Must not foul a track without proper authority or protection.

## Reporting for Duty (Rules 1–4)

- **1. General Orders, Bulletin Orders, Division Notices:** Must be read and understood before
  going on duty.
- **2. Standard Time:** Must use standard time; set watch before duty.
- **3. Correct Time:** Must obtain correct time; trains may not leave ahead of schedule.
- **4. Job Briefings:** Required before beginning work. Must cover: work to be performed, known
  hazards, protective measures, communication plan, emergency procedures.

## Miscellaneous Signals (Rules 10–24)

- **10–12:** Flags/lights required equipment; display periods; day vs. night signals.
- **13. Hand Signals:** Standard hand signals (stop, proceed, back up, reduce speed, apply/release
  brakes). Full table in references/.
- **16. Blue Signal Protection:** Equipment must not be moved when blue signal is applied.
  Only the employee who applied the blue signal may remove it. Requirements differ for main
  track/controlled siding vs. non-main track. See also `railroad-blue-signal`.
- **19. Engine Whistle/Horn Signals:** Standard horn sequences (stop, proceed, backup, grade
  crossing, flagman recall, etc.). Full table in references/.
- **20. Engine Bell:** Must be rung when moving through yards, approaching grade crossings.
- **22–24. Engine Lights / Markers:** Headlight requirements, dimming, failure procedure;
  required marker display for trains.

---

## Tampering (Rule 30)

Tampering with safety appliances, seals, or any device affecting safe operation is prohibited.
Must report any evidence of tampering immediately.

---

## Inspection of Equipment (Rules 70–73)

- **70. Inspection Before Departure:** Required at initial terminal and intermediate inspection
  points. Specific items enumerated (brakes, handbrakes, couplers, trucks, wheels, etc.).
- **71. Flat Spots:** (A) Normal speed if ≤2.5"; (B) 10 MPH if 2.5"–3"; (C) Remain stopped if >3"
  or hollow flange/shelled tread until repaired.
- **72. Inspection of Moving Trains:** Duty to observe passing trains for defects; specific
  notification procedures.
- **73. Bridges Without Walkways:** Inspection requirements when walkways are not available.
